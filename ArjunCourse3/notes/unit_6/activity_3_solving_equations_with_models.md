# VI-3 Patterns and Modeling: Solving Linear Equations with Models

[KEY]
A **bag** is the unknown (`x` cubes). A **cube** is 1. A balanced scale means the two sides are **equal**. Write the equation, then use **inverse operations on both sides**: take the same number of cubes off both pans, then split the remaining cubes equally among the bags. Example: 2 bags + 3 cubes = 7 cubes → `2x + 3 = 7` → subtract 3 → `2x = 4` → divide by 2 → **`x = 2`**.
[/KEY]

## Quick Review Notes

### Main Idea
Class packet **VI-3** (Course 3 Unit 6 topic **VI) Patterns and Modeling**): model a linear equation with **bags and cubes** on a balance scale, write the equation, then solve with inverse operations.

### Class method (same order as the worksheet)

1. **Read the scale.** Bags = `x` (same unknown in every bag). Loose cubes = 1 each.
2. **Write the equation.** Left pan total = right pan total.
3. **Undo the cubes first** (subtract the same number from both sides) so bags are alone with cubes.
4. **Undo the bags** (divide by the number of bags) to find cubes per bag.
5. **Check** by putting that many cubes in each bag and confirming both pans match.

### Important Formulas / Rules

| Scale picture | Equation | Inverse steps |
|---------------|----------|----------------|
| 2 bags and 3 cubes vs 7 cubes | `2x + 3 = 7` | −3, then ÷2 → `x = 2` |
| 3 bags vs 12 cubes | `3x = 12` | ÷3 → `x = 4` |
| 2 bags and 4 cubes vs 10 cubes | `2x + 4 = 10` | −4, then ÷2 → `x = 3` |

Whatever you take off one pan, take the **same** off the other pan so the scale stays balanced.

## Guided example (VI-3 front, Notes + problem 1)

**Diagram:** Left pan has **2 bags** and **3** loose cubes. Right pan has **7** cubes (a 2-by-3 block with one extra on top). The scale is balanced.

**Equation:** `2x + 3 = 7`

**Solve:**
1. Subtract 3 cubes from both pans: `2x = 4`.
2. Split the 4 cubes into the 2 bags: `x = 2`.

**Check:** Each bag has 2 cubes, so left pan is 2 + 2 + 3 = 7, matching the right pan.

**1. How many cubes must be in each bag?** **2**

The empty scales on the worksheet are for drawing those two steps (remove 3 cubes from both sides, then show 2 cubes in each bag).

## Model, write, solve (VI-3 front, problems 3–6)

**3.** 3 bags on one side, 12 cubes on the other.

- Picture: 3 bags vs 12 cubes.
- Equation: `3x = 12`
- Cubes per bag: **4** (because 3 × 4 = 12).

**4.** 2 bags and 4 cubes on one side, 10 cubes on the other.

- Picture: 2 bags + 4 cubes vs 10 cubes.
- Equation: `2x + 4 = 10`
- Subtract 4: `2x = 6`
- Divide by 2: **`x = 3`**
- Check: 2(3) + 4 = 10.

**5. Equations for #3 and #4**

| #3 | #4 |
|----|----|
| `3x = 12` | `2x + 4 = 10` |

**6. Inverse operations for #4**

`2x + 4 = 10`  
`2x + 4 − 4 = 10 − 4`  
`2x = 6`  
`2x ÷ 2 = 6 ÷ 2`  
`x = 3`

## Common slips
- Counting a **bag** as 1 cube instead of the unknown `x`.
- Subtracting cubes from only one side of the scale.
- Writing `2x = 10` for problem 4 and forgetting the 4 loose cubes.
- Dividing before subtracting (`2x + 4 = 10` is not `x + 4 = 5`).
