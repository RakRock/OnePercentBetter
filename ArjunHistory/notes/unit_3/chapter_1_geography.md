# 🐪 Chapter 1: The Ultimate Sandbox Trade

**Geography and the setup**

Imagine you live in a place with fields of gold, but no salt to preserve your food or stay hydrated. Now imagine travelers in a giant desert who have tons of rock salt and no gold. What do you do? You meet in the middle and trade.

## The three vegetation zones

West Africa is split into three main stripes:

1. **The desert (Sahara).** A massive ocean of hot sand, rich in rock salt mines such as **Taghaza**.
2. **The grassland (Sahel and savanna).** A flat, grassy zone good for cattle and farming. Empires built their cities here along the **Niger River**.
3. **The forest.** A wet, southern jungle loaded with secret gold mines such as **Wangara**.

## The desert super-vehicle

For a long time, the Sahara was too dangerous to cross. Then came the **Arabian camel**. Wide feet keep it from sinking in sand, and it can travel for days with very little water. Camels were the semi-trucks of this trade.

## The silent barter game

Traders often did not share a language, and gold miners wanted to keep the location of the mines secret. They used **silent barter**.

North Africans left salt slabs by a river, beat a drum, and walked away. Gold miners came forward, left an amount of gold, beat the drum, and stepped back. If both sides were happy with the size of the piles, they took the goods and left peacefully.
