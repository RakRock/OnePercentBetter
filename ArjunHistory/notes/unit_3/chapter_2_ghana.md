# 👑 Chapter 2: Ghana, the Middlemen

**About 500–1200 CE**

Think of the **Ghana Empire** as a toll booth on a popular bridge. Ghana did not own the gold mines, and it did not own the salt mines. It sat in the middle of the trade paths.

## Taxing the trade

The kings of Ghana built a large army with **iron weapons**. They told merchants: if you want to pass through safely, pay a tax in gold or salt. That tax made Ghana very rich.

## The king's gold rule

To keep gold from becoming cheap, the king made a law. Ordinary people could buy and trade **gold dust**. Every solid **gold nugget** found in the ground belonged to the king.

## A capital in two halves

Ghana's capital, **Koumbi Saleh**, was built in two parts. One half was for the traditional king and his court. The other half was a merchant town where Muslim traders built mosques and did business.
