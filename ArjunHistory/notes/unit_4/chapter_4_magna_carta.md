# 📜 Chapter 4: Limiting the King

**The Magna Carta**

For centuries, many European kings ruled as they pleased. If they wanted money, they took it. If they wanted someone in prison, they locked them up. In **1215 CE**, angry English nobles said that had to stop.

## The king and the barons

**King John** of England raised taxes very high and lost expensive wars. The nobles, called **barons**, marched toward London, cornered him at **Runnymede**, and forced him to sign the **Magna Carta**, the Great Charter.

## The law rules all

The Magna Carta said the king is not above the law. If the king wanted to raise taxes, he had to ask a council of nobles first.

It also set down a basic right later called **habeas corpus**: you get a trial by a jury of your peers before you can be locked away. That idea is an ancestor of later democratic thought, including the U.S. Constitution and the Bill of Rights.
