# 🔬 Chapter 5: Unlocking Achievements

**The Islamic Golden Age**

While much of Europe was going through a hard stretch sometimes called the Dark Ages, the Islamic world was in a **Golden Age** of science, math, and art. A lot of that work was centered in **Baghdad**.

## The House of Wisdom

The **House of Wisdom** was a great library and research center. Scholars collected books from Greece, Rome, India, and beyond, then translated them into Arabic so the knowledge would not be lost.

## Math and science

- **Al-Khwarizmi** wrote a book about solving equations. That work gave us the word **algebra**, from the Arabic *al-jabr*.
- Muslim scholars spread the numerals we use today — 0 through 9 — including **zero**, which they learned from India. These are often called Arabic numerals.
- **Ibn Sina** (Avicenna) wrote *The Canon of Medicine*, a medical textbook used for centuries.
- **Ibn al-Haytham** studied light and lenses. His field is **optics**.
- Sailors used the **astrolabe** to measure the stars, find their way, and face Mecca.
- Muslims learned **paper making** from China. Paper mills in Baghdad made books cheaper and easier to share.
- **Ibn Battuta** spent about 30 years traveling through Africa, Asia, and Europe and wrote down what he saw.

## Art and cities

Religious art usually avoided pictures of people and animals, so artists would not be seen as worshipping idols. Instead they mastered:

- **Calligraphy** — beautiful, artistic handwriting, often used for the Quran and mosque walls.
- **Arabesque** — repeating geometric and plant patterns.
- Buildings with grand **domes** and pointed **arches**.

Busy open-air markets were called **bazaars** or **souks**. Stories such as Aladdin and Sinbad come from *One Thousand and One Nights*, a collection that reflects the many cultures of the Abbasid world.
