# 👑 Chapter 4: The Expanding Empire and the Big Disagreement

After Muhammad died, the community needed a new leader. This leader was called a **caliph**, which means "successor." The first four caliphs are called the **Rightly Guided Caliphs**. The first of them, chosen by a group of early leaders, was **Abu Bakr**.

## The rapid growth

Under the caliphs, the Islamic Empire grew quickly. Within about 100 years, it stretched from India, across North Africa, and into Spain (called **Al-Andalus**).

- The **Umayyad** dynasty took power in **661 CE** and moved the capital to **Damascus**.
- The **Abbasid** dynasty overthrew the Umayyads in **750 CE** and built a new capital, **Baghdad**, on the Tigris River.
- **Arabic** became the language of government, trade, and religion.
- The **Battle of Tours** in **732 CE** stopped Muslim armies from pushing farther into Western Europe.
- In Spain, **Cordoba** became a famous center of learning. **Berber** armies from North Africa were a major part of the force that entered Spain.

Two tired empires, the **Byzantine** and **Persian** empires, had worn each other out. That made the early expansion easier.

## Why did it spread so fast?

- **Trade.** Muslim merchants traveled widely and shared their faith along with their goods. The empire connected routes from China to Europe.
- **People of the Book.** Christians and Jews were allowed to keep practicing their religions. They were called **dhimmi**, or "protected people," and paid a tax called the **jizya**. The Quran calls Jews and Christians "People of the Book" because they also follow scriptures about one God.
- Local cultures did not vanish. They blended with Arabic language and customs.

Law based on the Quran and the Sunnah is called **sharia**.

## The team split: Sunni and Shi'a

Early on, a huge argument broke out about who should lead next.

- **Sunnis** thought the community should choose the most qualified person. The word Sunni comes from following the **Sunnah**, the Prophet's tradition. Sunnis are the large majority of Muslims today. They see caliphs as human leaders who can make mistakes.
- **Shi'as** believed the leader should come from Muhammad's family, starting with his cousin and son-in-law, **Ali**. "Shi'a" means the party, or supporters, of Ali. They call their spiritual leaders **imams**.

In **680 CE**, the **Battle of Karbala** deepened the split. Muhammad's grandson **Hussein** was killed there. Shi'a Muslims remember him each year on **Ashura**.

Both branches read the same Quran and share the Five Pillars. The argument was about leadership, not about whether there is one God.

**Sufism** is a mystical path in Islam that focuses on a personal, direct experience of God through prayer, poetry, and meditation, rather than on who holds political power.
