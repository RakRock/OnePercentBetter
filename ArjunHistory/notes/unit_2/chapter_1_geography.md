# 🐪 Chapter 1: The Giant Sandbox

**The Geography of the Arabian Peninsula**

Imagine living in a sandbox the size of a country, where it is incredibly hot, water is as rare as gold, and there are no rivers to swim in. That was the Arabian Peninsula around the year 600 CE.

## The oasis lifeline

Because it was so dry, people lived near an **oasis** — a patch of green desert where underground water bubbles up to the surface. Oases let towns and farms grow in the middle of the desert.

## The Bedouins

**Bedouins** were nomadic desert travelers. They did not have permanent homes. They moved from place to place with their herds, lived in tents, and relied on camels — the "ships of the desert" — to survive. Camels could travel long distances with very little water.

People who settled in towns and farming oases are called **sedentary**. Traders who crossed the desert together for safety traveled in **caravans**.

## The ultimate crossroads

Even though it was a harsh desert, Arabia sat in the middle of the ancient world. It connected Asia, Africa, and Europe. Towns like **Mecca** became busy rest stops where traders bought and sold spices, silk, and perfumes.

- The **Red Sea** borders the western coast.
- The **Persian Gulf** borders the eastern side.
- **Medina** (then called Yathrib) was an oasis town north of Mecca, known for farming.
- Before Islam, the **Kaaba** in Mecca was a shrine that held statues of many gods. Mecca was already a major trade and religious center.
